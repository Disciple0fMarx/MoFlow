\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Conditional Flow Matching}
The baseline MoFlow architecture approximates a target vector field $U^t$ by learning a neural network $v_\theta$ that maps a Gaussian noise distribution to the true data distribution. For numerical stability, the model solves an equivalent data-space prediction task:
\begin{equation}
    D_\theta(Y^t, C, t) := Y^t + (1-t)v_\theta(Y^t, C, t)
\end{equation}
where $t \in [0, 1)$ represents the flow time, and $C$ represents the contextual information.

\subsection{Video-Conditioned Multimodal Fusion}
To extend this to a video-conditioned framework, we process an observed video clip $V \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{obs} \times H \times W \times 3}$. A global video encoder extracts scene-level context $z_{video\_global} \in \mathbb{R}^{L_{global} \times D}$. 

To combine this with the trajectory-social tokens $z_{traj}$, we employ a Cross-Attention fusion mechanism:
\begin{equation}
    z_{fused} = \text{CrossAttention}(Q=z_{traj}, K=z_{video}, V=z_{video})
\end{equation}
The resulting fused context modulates the generative flow field $v_\theta(Y^t, t | z_{traj}, z_{video})$, directly influencing the sampling of the $K$ diverse future trajectories.

\subsection{Agent-Centric Visual Conditioning}
In parallel to the global scene context, we also formulate an alternative agent-centric visual encoding strategy. Rather than extracting a single representation for the entire environment, this approach dynamically extracts localized visual patches from the video frames. 

For each agent $i$ at a given timestep, spatial coordinates are projected onto the pixel space using the scene's homography matrix. A visual patch $P_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H_p \times W_p \times 3}$ is cropped around the agent's mapped location. A dedicated convolutional encoder then processes these patches to yield agent-specific visual tokens $z_{video\_local}^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$. 

This localized formulation aims to capture fine-grained environmental constraints immediately surrounding the pedestrian (e.g., adjacent obstacles or physical boundaries). The resulting local tokens can be fused with the agent's corresponding trajectory tokens, offering a highly localized modulation of the generative flow field. The success of this approach is strictly dependent on the precision of the world-to-pixel coordinate transformations.
